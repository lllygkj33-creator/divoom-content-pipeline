## **When Were Baby Monitors Invented?**

Baby monitors have been around since 1937. The first introduced is the _Zenith Radio Nurse_. The concept emerged following the tragic Lindbergh baby abduction, raising parents’ awareness of the importance of protecting their kids.

Eugene McDonald, the head of the Zenith Radio Corporation, desired a device that would enable him to hear his child from another room. He applied radio technology to develop the first monitor and partnered with Isamu Noguchi to create the end product.

Because of this work, the first non-visual baby monitor was developed. It relied on basic radio signals to transmit the sound from your baby's room to a receiver. It was a big step forward, and made parents feel comfortable despite being far.

## **Why Was the First Baby Monitor Invented?**

The first baby monitor was inspired by the tragic Lindbergh baby kidnapping. In 1932, the young son of Charles Lindbergh was abducted from his home. There were people in the house then. The case horrified the nation and focused attention on the safety of children.

That disturbing event gave birth to an idea in the mind of Eugene McDonald, president of Zenith Radio Corporation. As a concerned father, he was motivated to find a way to protect his child and be able to hear his daughter from another room. After watching it operate, he felt every parent should be able to have that peace of mind. This is how the first baby monitor came to be.

Baby monitors started as simple one-way audio devices. Later, it improved in reliability and safety with better signals. By the 1970s, monitors could let parents talk back and even alert them to breathing problems. This sets the stage for modern and high-tech baby monitors.

## **The Shift from Radio Waves to Digital Encryption (2000s)**

Have you ever wondered how parents first began watching over their babies from another room? In the 1990s, video baby monitors surged in popularity as cameras allowed parents to check in on their children from afar. Plus, news coverage of caregiver abuse made these new “nanny cams” an essential tool for peace of mind.

As technology developed, monitors began employing Wi-Fi, DECT, and FHSS technology. They stopped using outdated analog signals by the early 2000s. This resulted in a more dependable and safer audio and video.

Based on these enhancements, during the 2010s, Wi-Fi monitors became less expensive and more mainstream. Well-known models provided high-quality video and sound, and a growing number of monitors integrated with smart home platforms such as Apple HomeKit, Amazon Alexa, and Google Home. That way, parents could easily watch and interact with their babies using apps and smart devices.

You can also monitor the temperature and humidity in the room, play lullabies, and even keep track of your baby’s sleep and health.
