### Count the Hardest Moments

The easiest way to narrow your options is to count the moments when the stroller has to “work” during a trip. For a long-distance family visit, that might mean folding it at home, lifting it into the car, opening it at a rest stop, folding it again at a relative’s house, storing it in a hallway, and repeating the whole thing on the way back.

If you fly, add airport security, gate-checking, overhead bin rules, rental cars, hotel elevators, and crowded family gatherings. A stroller can feel wonderful on a smooth sidewalk but become frustrating if it takes two hands, a foot, and a prayer to fold while your baby is tired.

![Hands folding a stroller at an airport gate with luggage nearby](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0559/2321/2486/files/Hands_folding_a_stroller_at_an_airport_gate_with_l.png?v=1784956211)

### Match It to the Destination, Too

Think about where your family lives. A compact stroller may be perfect if you visit relatives in an apartment with an elevator and limited storage. If they live in a suburban neighborhood with gravel driveways, grass, or uneven sidewalks, you may want sturdier wheels, better suspension, and a little more seat comfort.

I also like to think about the “parking spot.” Will the stroller sit by the front door, in a guest room closet, in the garage, or in your car trunk all weekend? If the stroller blocks a walkway every time someone comes in with groceries, it will feel annoying fast.

## Pick the Right Stroller Type for Your Baby’s Age

Your baby’s age matters more than many parents expect. A newborn has very different stroller needs than a 10-month-old who can sit up and snack in the seat while cousins run around the yard.

### For Newborns and Young Infants

For babies younger than about 6 months, look for a stroller that offers a near-flat recline, a bassinet option, or an approved infant car seat compatibility. Young babies who cannot sit independently need more support, and a company notes that newborns generally need a full recline, flat seat, bassinet attachment, or compatible infant car seat setup for stroller use from birth through the early months [newborns](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Carriages-and-Strollers) [through](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Carriages-and-Strollers) [6](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Carriages-and-Strollers) [months](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Carriages-and-Strollers).

This is where a travel system can be genuinely helpful. A travel system includes an infant car seat, base, and compatible stroller, so you can move a sleeping baby from car to stroller without unbuckling them for every short stop.

### For Older Babies and Toddlers

Once your baby is older than 6 months and sitting well, you usually have more flexibility. A lightweight travel stroller, compact stroller, or umbrella-style stroller may be enough for visiting family, especially if your child mostly needs a place to rest during errands, airport lines, family outings, or neighborhood walks.

The tradeoff is comfort and terrain. Lightweight strollers are often easier to carry and fold, but they may have smaller wheels, less storage, thinner padding, and less ability to handle rough sidewalks or bumpy paths. That is fine for a quick airport trip, but less ideal if every visit includes long walks, outdoor festivals, or older cousins’ soccer games.

## Check the Features That Matter on Family Trips

For flights, choose a stroller that folds as small as possible and check your airline’s rules before the trip. Some compact strollers may fit in an overhead bin, but airline policies and bin sizes vary, so gate-checking may still be the more realistic plan overhead bin.

Think through the airport without assuming you will have two free hands. If you are traveling solo with a baby, a diaper backpack, and a car seat, a lighter stroller may matter more than a bigger basket. If another adult is with you, you may have more room to choose comfort over the absolute smallest fold.

## Practical Next Steps

The best stroller for frequent long-distance family visits is usually the one that handles your most annoying travel moments well. If you have a newborn and drive often, a travel system or car seat-compatible stroller can make transfers easier. If your child is older and you fly or pack tight, a lightweight compact stroller may be the better everyday travel helper.

Before buying, test these five things in real life:

- Fold and unfold it at least five times in a row.
- Lift it into your trunk with one hand or while wearing your diaper bag.
- Check that it fits with your usual overnight gear.
- Put your actual diaper bag in the basket.
- Confirm the seat setup works for your baby’s current age, weight, and stage.

For most families, the sweet spot is not the fanciest stroller. It is the one you can fold quickly, lift comfortably, store easily, and use safely when everyone is tired after a long trip.
