Whether you want something permanent or more flexible, selecting the right changing station furniture will help you create a functional and non-cluttering diaper changing environment.

### Dresser Top Changing Stations

If you have a sturdy dresser, adding a changing surface as a changing station is often the simplest solution. You can find a variety of dressers with an appropriate changing surface for your needs, or install one on the top of your existing furniture that matches the manufacturer’s recommendations.

### Wall Mounted Or Fold Down Changing Stations

If you have a limited amount of space, consider getting a wall mounted or fold down changing station. It will save you precious space and give you exactly what you need, a changing station. Make sure to install it according to the manufacturer instructions or call a professional for help if you have any problems.

### Rolling Cart Changing Stations

A rolling cart combined with an independent surface designed for changing is another flexible option. You will be able to conveniently transport your changing supplies around the house and have plenty of storage space to accommodate your needs. Make sure that the cart you select is compatible with the changing surface you will use as a station. This will prevent potential injuries in the future.

### Portable Changing Basket Or Kit

If you wish to avoid permanent installations, a portable changing basket or kit is the way to go. You can save space inside a cabinet, a closet, or other built-in storage solutions while having a diaper changing surface available for use. Combined with an independent and approved changing surface, such a setup will allow you to keep your home organized and clutter-free.

## **Compare the Four Layouts Before You Buy**

Choosing the right changing station is about more than identifying what's included: assess each layout based on their ability to meet your storage needs while maintaining functionality and accessibility. Often, a simplified formula provides better value: a layout that makes the room easier to navigate is a better choice than one that has more storage capacity.

### Dresser Top Changing Station

This type of changing station is often recommended for smaller rooms as it combines two types of storage (dresser and diaper changing) in one space-saving unit. This way, you can also have an option to remove the changing station when it's no longer necessary.

### Wall Mounted or Fold Down Changing Station

If you're looking for a changing station in a very small room, consider a wall mounted or a fold down changing station. Both these options offer the ability to save space by keeping the changing surface out of view when it's not in use. These options, however, offer fewer storage options than the ones mentioned above and require careful installation.

### Rolling Cart Storage

This type of storage is best for keeping the diaper changing equipment organized and within reach. It allows you to transport the inventory from one room to another as needed and offers a flexible storage solution. Rolling carts, however, should not be used as a changing station unless they're specifically designed for this purpose.

### Portable Changing Basket or Compact Kit

These kits offer the most space-saving option as they can be tucked away in a variety of enclosed spaces such as cabinets, closets, or shelves. The main advantage of a portable basket is the ability to keep your diaper changing equipment secure, organized, and within reach. This option is best suited for those who have a compact but safe changing surface and wish to store their inventory in a separate, enclosed space.

### Choose the Layout Based on the Space You Have

## **Use Vertical Storage Without Creating a Reach Hazard**

Vertical storage is a great way to optimize the limited space in a small nursery or bedroom, but it's important to plan accordingly and make sure everything is safely stored at a reachable height to prevent accidents. By using wall mounted storage you can easily free up much needed living space, as long as the most used items can be reached and the storage is not placed overhead as to cause potential safety issues.

### Install Safe Wall Mounted Storage

Shelves or other storage should be mounted in a secure manner and used to store only light objects such as packages of diapers that have not yet been opened, folded up burp cloths, extra changing pad covers, or receiving blankets.

Light items that are not necessary to have on hand at all times should be placed overhead in secure storage, and all storage should be mounted securely according to the manufacturer's recommendations.

### Avoid Overhead Storage Over The Changing Surface

Baskets, shelves, jars, or other storage that are mounted or placed on the overhead or above the changing surface need to be avoided even if they are mounted or placed securely. All storage should be mounted or placed in a way that avoids having to bend down, lean over, or stretch while holding your baby

### Keep Frequently Used Supplies Within Easy Reach

Diapers, wipes, diaper cream, or other supplies that are used frequently should be stored within easy reach at a comfortable height while standing at the changing station. You will want to have everything within easy reach because it will help you avoid leaning or stretching when your baby is needing you the most.

### Update Storage As Your Child Grows

As children grow, become more active, and mobile, it is important to make sure that potentially dangerous objects are stored out of their reach in the nursery or baby's room. Medications, creams, nail clippers, cleaning supplies, and other potentially dangerous small objects need to be stored safely and securely

## **Give a Rolling Cart a Parking Bay**

A rolling cart can be a great space-saving storage solution, but it has to have a home. It will help you to have everything organized and within reach while keeping your home clutter-free.

### Pick a Convenient Parking Spot

The cart should be parked in an area where it does not obstruct any closet doors, dresser drawers, or your path from the changing station to the crib or into the room. You should have enough space to navigate the room without having to move the cart first.

### Lock the Wheels

If your rolling cart has wheels that can be locked or a brake, make sure to engage it and lock the wheels when not in use. It will ensure that the cart does not roll away from the parking spot on its own.

### Set Everything in Order

Store frequently used things, such as diapers and wipes, on the top shelf or somewhere within easy reach. Place backups and less used items on the lower shelves, and keep the cart's space free from unused large packs or rolls of anything.

### Test Your Parking Spot Before Getting Used to It

Before you start using the rolling cart as a regular storage solution, make sure to move it a couple of times from the parking spot to the changing station and back to check that it does not obstruct your traffic in any way.

### Remember That a Rolling Cart Is Meant to Store, Not to Change

A rolling cart is only a storage unit for your diaper-changing needs. It is crucial to keep your baby on a reliable and safe changing station or a changing table unless it is a special-purpose rolling cart.

## **Separate Working Supplies From Bulk Refills**

A well organized changing station is not about having more things within reach. It is about having only the things that you need within reach while keeping everything else out of the way. Keeping your changing station and your supply stash organized by dividing them into separate storage areas is going to make your job so much easier.

### Working Layer

The working layer is where all the materials for one diaper change should be placed. This can be a shallow drawer or a couple of small bins or even a small organizer right next to the changing area. Keeping these supplies organized also makes it easier to monitor your baby's diaper habits and notice changes over time. - Diaper cream

### Refill Layer

## **Run a Five Minute Clearance and Safety Test**

Before you consider your job complete, test it out for a few minutes. A quick walk-through can help identify things that might not be immediately apparent.

Test the primary walkways by walking through your space a few times while pushing or pulling a weighted object similar in weight to your little one. Make sure all doors, drawers, and cabinets are open and free of obstruction. It can be tempting to ignore these little details, but they will quickly become apparent in the middle of a busy night.

Walk around your changing station in your regular diaper-changing position, facing the storage area. This will allow you access to the most common storage areas without straining. Make sure nothing requires you to twist around or ask for help.

Complete a quick safety check by confirming:

- Furniture is properly anchored when required.
- The changing product is compatible with the furniture it is installed on.
- Rolling cart brakes engage securely.
- Wall shelves and hooks remain firmly attached.
- Loose items cannot fall onto the changing surface or into the baby's sleeping area.
- Walkways remain clear of baskets, cords, and storage containers.

As your baby learns to crawl, walk, and explore his surroundings, it is important that you go through this same safety review. As your home arrangement changes, you may need to make adjustments to ensure that the baby changing station remains practical and safe.

## **FAQs**

### Can I use a dresser as a changing station in a small room?

Yes. A durable dresser that provides an area for changing along with appropriate infant or toddler changing products will be best in case of space issue because it can be used for two purposes and will allow maximizing the space. This furniture will also provide a place for child after another stage of development when they will not need a changing table anymore. It is essential to make sure that the dressers and changing products are compatible with each other to ensure safety and proper usage.

### Is a rolling cart enough for a baby changing station?

No. A rolling cart can hold diapers and wipes, but it should not be used to replace an approved changing surface unless it is designed specifically for use as a changing station by the manufacturer. A cart can be used for storing items in an organized manner that may need to be accessed at multiple locations within the room. To reduce the risk of injury to the infant and caregiver, always place the cart in a designated location and use an approved changing surface that has been recommended by the manufacturer for preparing the infant for a diapering procedure.

### How much floor space does a small changing station need?

There is no universal measurement, because it depends on the room where the furniture is going to be placed. It is better to measure the area to be able to leave enough space to move around and reach for objects within the room. It is important to leave enough space to let the child move freely and without blocking the central passage. The ideal number of square feet will eliminate the clutter, while providing comfort and safety in diaper changing.

### Where should a diaper pail go in a small nursery?

Place the diaper pail at the caregivers' exit side of the changing station so that it is within easy reach once your baby is secured. You should place the pail out of the way so that it is not an obstruction to your primary area in your workspace. Avoid placing it beneath shelves, near heat sources, or in other locations that are unsafe or make it hard to access. By putting the pail in the right place, you make diaper changes faster and easier while also keeping your clean supplies separated from your dirty ones.

### How can I add storage without putting shelves above the baby?

Use available wall space on the wall near the changer area to install shelves, hooks, or pockets in which to safely store smaller items so they are out of the way of the changing area. Store larger supply items overhead and those used most often within easy reach (below shoulder level). Do not place heavy items or ones with small parts above the baby's reach where the child might pull them down. As your child becomes more independent and mobile, place small objects, lotions, and cleaning supplies in child-resistant containers.

### Do I need a changing station in every room?

No. Most people I know have one good changing station well-stocked with necessary items. If you need a place to change a baby that's not the main one, a small portable changing bag or something will have enough to do the task until you get back to your main station. It's much more convenient and avoids clutter and extra stockpiling.
