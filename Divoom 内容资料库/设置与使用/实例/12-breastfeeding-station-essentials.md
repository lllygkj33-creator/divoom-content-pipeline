## **What Is a Breastfeeding Station (and Why You'll Be Glad You Made One)**

A breastfeeding station is the main spot where all the feeding materials, as well as some other necessities for your baby and yourself, are kept. This station can be located either near the couch, by your bed, or in the nursery – wherever you usually feed the baby.

Babies drink milk eight to twelve times a day. If you need to go back to bed multiple times throughout the day to collect some water, some food, pads or diapers, then that gets really tiring.

Having everything you need within reach is going to make the feeding process much easier for you and prevent unnecessary walking around your home, especially in the late hours of the night.

You don’t have to complicate matters. That is what this station is all about – keeping everything you need within reach.

## **Cart vs. Caddy vs. Basket: Which One Should You Choose?**

There isn't a single "right" setup. The ideal option will depend on your house, your daily schedule, and how frequently you change rooms.

### Rolling Cart

A rolling cart has more advantages if you need more storage or pump a lot. Its many shelves make it easy to separate feeding supplies, self-care items, and baby essentials.

### Breastfeeding Caddy

The purpose of a breastfeeding caddy is portability.

A light caddy with durable handles helps you move your supplies from one room to another easily when you are feeding in several rooms in a day.

The breastfeeding caddy include nursing pads, burp cloths, snacks, wipes, diapers, and a phone charger.

### Breastfeeding Basket

If you always breastfeed in the same location, for instance near the bedside, then the use of a breastfeeding basket will be ideal for you.

All the basic necessities that you need while nursing (such as water, food, burp cloths, nipple cream, and nursing pads) will be ideal contents for a breastfeeding basket.

### A Quick Way to Decide

- A cart - if you want a single permanent feeding station or pump frequently.
- A caddy - if you move around the house frequently.
- A basket - if you have limited space and feed primarily in one room.

Eventually, it turns out that many parents can benefit from having two smaller stations (a caddy in the living room and a basket near the bed).

- Water bottle
- Snacks

- Nursing pads

- Lip balm

## **Breastfeeding Cart Organization: How to Organize Your Station Like a Pro**

Whether you're using a cart, caddy, or basket, a little organization goes a long way.

For a rolling cart, arrange supplies by how often you use them.

- Top shelf: Feeding or pumping supplies
- Middle shelf: Water, snacks, charger, and self-care items
- Bottom shelf: Diapers, wipes, and extra clothes

Moreover, you must make it a routine for yourself to fill up your station. At the end of the day, it is necessary that you top up on all the essentials from your water bottles to your snacks to your diapers and wipes. Moreover, you must conduct stock-taking of your items every week.

It is not necessary for your breastfeeding station to be perfect. It does its job perfectly fine as long as it keeps all the basics within your reach.

## **Setup Ideas by Lifestyle and Room**

Your breastfeeding station should fit your home and routine. Here are a few setup ideas.

### Small Apartments

If you're living in a not-so-spacious home, keep a basket beside your bed and a portable caddy for daytime use. This gives you easy access to your essentials without taking up much room.

### Multi-Level Homes

Rather than moving around the equipment upstairs and downstairs, make sure to have a station upstairs and downstairs. Both stations will just need the basic equipment like water, snacks, burp cloths, diapers, and wipes.

### Nighttime Feeding Station

For overnight feeds, place your station next to the bed and include:

- Water bottle
- Phone charger
- Burp cloths
- Nursing pads
- Snacks
- A dimmable night light

Keeping everything nearby makes those middle-of-the-night wake-ups a little less disruptive.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### What is the difference between a nursing cart, caddy, and basket?

A nursing cart has more shelves and is a big rolling unit that is perfect for the pumping or stationary feeding locations. The caddy can be carried around because of the inbuilt handles, while the basket is simply the basic choice to have everything in one place.

### What are the must-have breastfeeding station essentials?

Necessities that should be carried along during breastfeeding include water, food, burp clothes, nursing pads, nipple cream, diapers, wipes, phone charger, and nursing pillow. If you are used to pumping your breast milk, also bring along your stuff.

### How often should I restock my breastfeeding basket or caddy?

Check your station daily to refill snacks, water, diapers, and wipes. A weekly restock is a good time to reorganize supplies and replace anything that's running low. As for the basket itself, refill it during your baby’s daytime naps. That ensures you’ll not end empty-handed when you suddenly need something.

### Can I use a regular basket instead of a rolling cart?

The regular basket will work great if you generally feed in one room without needing any additional space for the pump stuff. You can always upgrade to the cart later on if you need it.

### Where is the best place to set up a breastfeeding station?

Choose the place where you feed your baby most often, whether that's the nursery, living room, or bedside. If you regularly feed in different rooms, consider setting up two smaller stations instead of one.

Firstly, take care of the basics. Design your station depending on your daily routine and adjust as per your baby's requirements. The best breastfeeding station is not the one that is very complicated but the one that makes it easy to feed the baby regularly.
