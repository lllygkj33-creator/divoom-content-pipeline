## Quick Action Checklist

1. Build a safe sleep space first: back sleeping, firm flat surface, fitted sheet only, nothing soft in the sleep area ( [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/A-Parents-Guide-to-Safe-Sleep.aspx), [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/SafeSleep)).
2. Use the monitor as an alert system, not a diagnosis tool; always verify concerns by checking your baby in person.
3. Keep monitor cords out of reach and at least 3 ft from the crib/bassinet/play yard ( [Safe to](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ) [Sleep](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ) [FAQ](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ)).
4. If you feed in bed and doze off, move baby back to their separate sleep space as soon as you wake up ( [Safe to Sleep FAQ](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ)).
5. Keep baby from overheating: usually one more clothing layer than you wear, and watch for sweating or a hot chest ( [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/A-Parents-Guide-to-Safe-Sleep.aspx)).

## Room Temperature and White Noise, Without Overthinking

Aim for a room that feels comfortable to a lightly dressed adult, then dress baby in about one extra layer. If baby is sweaty, flushed, or has a hot chest, scale layers back ( [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/A-Parents-Guide-to-Safe-Sleep.aspx)).

White noise can be useful for masking household sounds, but volume and distance matter. Testing of infant white-noise devices found that many exceeded high noise thresholds when set to max volume and placed very close (about 4 inches), with risk rising when sound is loud and near the baby ( [Hong](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165587621001506) [et al., 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165587621001506)). Practical rule: keep it low and place it across the room, not in or right next to the crib.

## What Is Common vs. What Is a Red Flag

**Common at night**

- Baby is noisy in sleep (brief grunts, wiggles, short fussing).
- You get occasional monitor connection or motion alerts.
- You still need a few in-person checks on harder nights.

**Red flags to act on now**

- Unsafe sleep setup: soft bedding, incline, couch/chair sleep, or bed-sharing.
- Cords or monitor accessories within baby’s reach.
- Relying on a monitor claim that it can prevent SIDS.
- Baby seems in distress, hard to wake, or has obvious breathing/color changes: seek emergency care right away.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a smart monitor prevent SIDS?**

**A:** No. Home heart/breathing monitors are not recommended as a primary way to detect SIDS, and no monitor is authorized to prevent SIDS/SUID ( [Safe to](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ) [Sleep](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ) [FAQ](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ), [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/do-not-use-unauthorized-infant-devices-monitoring-vital-signs-fda-safety-communication)).

**Q: If the monitor looks normal, do I still need to follow safe sleep rules every time?**

**A:** Yes. Monitor readings never replace a safe sleep environment or adult supervision ( [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/do-not-use-unauthorized-infant-devices-monitoring-vital-signs-fda-safety-communication), [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/A-Parents-Guide-to-Safe-Sleep.aspx)).

**Q: What should I do if I accidentally fall asleep while feeding in bed?**

**A:** As soon as you wake up, move baby back to a separate safe sleep space near your bed ( [Safe](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ) [to Sleep FAQ](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/FAQ)).
